 PART 1 — UNDERSTAND THE WHOLE THING
=======================================================================

1. THE PROBLEM (what SEBI's problem statement says, in plain words)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
SEBI regulates India's stock markets. It writes rules for "market
intermediaries" — stock brokers, investment advisers, mutual funds. The
rules arrive as PDFs called CIRCULARS. A "master circular" is the big
consolidated rulebook for one type of firm; the Master Circular for
Stock Brokers is basically the entire rulebook a broker must follow.

SEBI's problem statement names two pains:

  Pain 1 — when SEBI issues or changes a rule, a human must read the
  PDF, work out whether it applies to their firm, work out which
  internal processes it touches, and update them. Slow, manual, and
  different firms read the same rule differently.

  Pain 2 — compliance teams must track ALL existing obligations, keep
  PROOF each one is being fulfilled, and produce audit trails when SEBI
  inspects. Small teams have the most gaps.

Root cause, in SEBI's own framing: regulation is written as TEXT for
humans, but compliance systems need STRUCTURED, machine-checkable rules.
Bridging that gap is the "core unsolved problem."

They asked for a solution demonstrated on one intermediary type and one
rulebook, and suggested the Master Circular for Stock Brokers. That is
exactly what we picked.

2. OUR SOLUTION — PONEGLYPH
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
(The name: in One Piece, poneglyphs are ancient stones carrying the true
history, written in a script almost nobody can read. SEBI circulars are
our poneglyphs — dense text only specialists can read. Our engine reads
them.)

One line: Poneglyph turns SEBI's rulebook into a living, self-updating
to-do list for a specific broker — where every item can prove itself.

The loop the engine runs:

  ONBOARD    resolve the firm from public filings, work out what
             business it actually runs, and compute which parts of the
             rulebook bind IT specifically.
  WATCH      a scraper agent polls SEBI's website daily and catches
             every new circular.
  DECIDE     an applicability agent rules whether each catch binds our
             firm — yes / no / partly, quoting the clause that decides.
  EXTRACT    an extraction agent pulls out each OBLIGATION, anchored to
             the exact paragraph and character span of the circular.
  VERIFY     a deterministic checker (plain code, no AI) validates every
             proposal: do citations resolve? do deadlines parse?
  HUMAN GATE a named compliance officer approves before anything enters
             the register. Agents propose; humans sign.
  EVIDENCE   each obligation binds to PROOF: an uploaded document, a
             data-check against a system of record, or a live security
             scan. Everything hashed and timestamped.
  GAP → TASK no proof means a GAP, and the engine spawns a remediation
             task with an owner and a deadline from the circular itself.
  DIFF       when SEBI AMENDS, the engine diffs old vs new and re-maps
             ONLY what changed.
  AUDIT      every action lands in a hash-chained log — each entry's
             fingerprint includes the previous one, so tampering with
             history is mathematically visible.

The positioning line that wins the room:
  "ONE OBLIGATION GRAPH, TWO WINDOWS." The broker's compliance team
  manages compliance through one window. SEBI verifies through the
  other — a read-only Inspector view of the same data, plus an MCP
  endpoint so SEBI's own AI agents can query compliance directly
  instead of asking for screenshots.

3. THE THESIS THAT MAKES US DIFFERENT (say this at least twice)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Everyone else builds a "circular → obligation table" chatbot. The
register is the easy half. Here is the half nobody does:

  TWO BROKERS HOLDING THE SAME LICENCE DO NOT OWE THE SAME THINGS.

A broker's obligations depend on what it actually IS — its size, its
segments, its designations. Angel One runs margin trading, algo
trading and a depository participant business, and its scale crosses
the Qualified Stock Broker thresholds. A small broker with the same
stock-broker licence and none of those things owes materially less.

So Poneglyph does not start from a checklist. It starts from the FIRM.
Onboarding builds an entity profile, the profile decides which Parts of
the rulebook bind, and those Parts generate the document requests. Change
the profile and the whole register changes underneath you. On screen:
Angel One draws 26 document asks; a same-licence peer without QSB, MTF
or algo trading would draw 22.

If a judge remembers one sentence from you, make it that one.

5. HOW WE GOT HERE (the research, if anyone asks)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1 — We read the problem statement and caught our own bias. Our
background is cybersecurity, but this PS is about FINANCIAL-regulatory
compliance. The broker rulebook is overwhelmingly client-money, margin,
KYC and grievance rules; cyber is one Part of ten. So we deliberately
de-centred cyber: live security scans are ONE of three evidence
connectors, not the headline.

Step 2 — We went to the primary source. We read the actual Table of
Contents of SEBI's Master Circular for Stock Brokers
(SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD-1/P/CIR/2024/53) rather than trusting a
summary — which matters, because a widely-mirrored PDF with a similar
name is actually the DEBT circular, and an AI summary of it invented a
plausible-looking broker index. Our taxonomy is the real ten Parts.

Step 3 — We researched a live scenario. On 3 July 2026 SEBI amended
Para 46 of the Master Circular, replacing how brokers handle clients'
unpaid securities with a pledge-based system called CUSPA. Every broker
in India is working through it right now. That is our hero scenario.

Step 4 — We researched how serious companies architect this. Norm Ai
(US, $100M+ raised, compliance agents for asset managers) compiles
regulations into structured logic. Anthropic's "Building Effective
Agents" guidance says: in production, use a fixed pipeline with AI only
at the judgment steps — never free-roaming agents. That is exactly our
architecture: A NEW REGULATION IS A NEW JOB THROUGH A FIXED PIPELINE,
not a new agent. Agents are disposable workers; the schema is the
permanent thing; a deterministic verifier checks everything; a human
signs. It is the only architecture a regulator can audit.

6. THE TEN PARTS (know these — it is our credibility)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
SEBI's Master Circular for Stock Brokers is organised in ten Parts. Our
register mirrors them exactly, with the verbatim titles:

   I    Registration of Stock Brokers                    3 obligations
   II   Supervision & Oversight (system audit, QSB)      2
   III  Dealings with Client        (the biggest Part)  19
   IV   Technology Related Provisions (CSCRF, algo)      6
   V    Change in Status, Constitution, Control          2
   VI   Foreign Accounts Tax Compliance Act              1
   VII  Investor Grievance Redressal (SCORES, ODR)       3
   VIII Default Related Provisions                       0  ← on purpose
   IX   Miscellaneous (outsourcing, books, upstreaming)  5
   X    Reporting Requirements                           2
                                                       ─────
                                                        43

PART VIII SHOWS ZERO AND THAT IS A FEATURE. Default provisions bind only
when a member actually defaults. The engine evaluated it, ruled it
event-driven, and put it under a standing trigger watch — the reason is
on the record. If a judge points at the zero, that is a gift:

  "That zero is a decision, not a gap. Default provisions only bind on a
   default event, so the engine excluded the Part and filed the reason.
   An unexplained zero would be a hole — an explained one is the product
   working."

Two frameworks sit ACROSS the Parts and decide how heavily they land:
  · CSCRF — Cybersecurity & Cyber Resilience Framework (Aug 2024),
    graded by entity size.
  · QSB — Qualified Stock Broker. SEBI loads high-impact brokers with
    enhanced obligations because their failure would be systemic.
    Parameters: active clients, client assets, trading volumes, end-of-
    day margin obligations, plus proprietary volumes and compliance and
    grievance scores added in 2024.

7. MINI GLOSSARY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Circular        — a SEBI rule document (PDF).
Master Circular — the consolidated rulebook for one firm type.
Part            — one of the ten sections of that rulebook.
Obligation      — one thing the firm must do, extracted from a clause.
Control         — the firm's internal process that satisfies it.
Evidence        — proof the control ran (document / data-check / scan).
Document        — source material the engine LEARNS THE FIRM FROM.
                  Not the same as evidence — see the Document Vault.
Gap             — an obligation with no evidence. Orange in the UI.
Remediation     — the task that closes a gap.
Audit trail     — the append-only, hash-chained log of every action.
Agentic         — AI agents doing multi-step work (read → decide → act).
MCP             — Model Context Protocol; a standard port through which
                  any AI assistant can query a system. Ours is read-only.
QSB             — Qualified Stock Broker (see above).
CSCRF           — SEBI's cybersecurity framework.
CUSPA           — "Client Unpaid Securities Pledgee Account". The new
                  rule: when a client hasn't fully paid for shares, the
                  broker no longer moves them to its own account — they
                  stay with the client, PLEDGED to the broker. Client
                  must be told by email/SMS; if unpaid after 5 trading
                  days the pledge is released on day 6; daily
                  reconciliation required. This is the Para 46 amendment.
