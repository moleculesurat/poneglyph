 PART 1 — UNDERSTAND THE WHOLE THING IN 10 MINUTES
=======================================================================

1. THE PROBLEM (what SEBI's problem statement says, in plain words)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
SEBI is the regulator of India's stock markets. It writes rules for
"market intermediaries" — stock brokers, investment advisers, mutual
funds, depositories. The rules arrive as PDF documents called
CIRCULARS. A "master circular" is the big consolidated rulebook for
one type of firm — the Master Circular for Stock Brokers is basically
the entire rulebook a broker must follow.

SEBI's own problem statement says there are two pains:

  Pain 1 — when SEBI issues or changes a rule, a human has to read the
  PDF, figure out whether it applies to their firm, figure out which
  internal processes it touches, and update them. This is slow, manual
  and different firms interpret the same rule differently.

  Pain 2 — compliance teams must track ALL existing obligations, keep
  PROOF that each one is being fulfilled, and produce audit trails when
  SEBI inspects them. Small firms with tiny compliance teams have the
  most gaps.

The root cause, in SEBI's own framing: regulation is written as TEXT
for humans, but compliance systems need STRUCTURED, machine-checkable
rules. Bridging that gap is the "core unsolved problem."

They asked for: a tech solution that turns regulatory text into
operational compliance action, demonstrated on one intermediary type
and one rulebook. They even suggested the corpus: the Master Circular
for Stock Brokers. That is exactly what we picked.

2. OUR SOLUTION — PONEGLYPH
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
(The name: in One Piece, poneglyphs are ancient stones carrying the
true history, written in a script almost nobody can read. SEBI
circulars are our poneglyphs — dense text only specialists can read.
Our engine reads them.)

One line: Poneglyph turns SEBI's rulebook into a living, self-updating
to-do list for a broker — where every item can prove itself.

The loop the engine runs, in order:

  WATCH      a scraper agent polls SEBI's website daily and catches
             every new circular.
  DECIDE     an "applicability" agent reads each catch and rules:
             does this bind OUR firm? yes / no / partially — with the
             exact quoted clause as its reason.
  EXTRACT    if it applies, an extraction agent pulls out each
             OBLIGATION (a thing the firm must do), anchored to the
             exact paragraph and line of the circular.
  VERIFY     a deterministic checker (plain code, no AI) validates
             every agent proposal: do the citations resolve? do the
             deadlines parse? Nothing hallucinated gets through.
  HUMAN GATE a human compliance officer approves before anything
             enters the register. Agents propose; humans sign.
  EVIDENCE   each obligation gets bound to PROOF: an uploaded document,
             a data-check against another system, or a live security
             scan. Everything is hashed and timestamped.
  GAP → TASK if there is no proof, that's a GAP, and the engine spawns
             a remediation task with an owner and a deadline taken
             from the circular itself.
  DIFF       when SEBI AMENDS a circular, the engine diffs old vs new
             and re-maps ONLY what changed. This is the hero demo.
  AUDIT      every single action is written to a hash-chained log —
             each entry's fingerprint includes the previous entry's,
             so tampering with history is mathematically visible.

And the positioning line that wins the room:
  "ONE OBLIGATION GRAPH, TWO WINDOWS." The broker's compliance team
  manages compliance through one window. SEBI verifies it through the
  other — a read-only Inspector view of the same data, plus an MCP
  endpoint so SEBI's own AI agents can query compliance directly
  instead of asking for screenshots.

3. HOW WE GOT HERE (the research we did before building)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1 — We read the problem statement carefully and caught our own
bias. Our company (Walrus Securitas) does CYBER security. But this PS
is about FINANCIAL-regulatory compliance: the broker rulebook is ~90%
client-money rules, margin rules, KYC, grievances — and only ~10%
cyber. So we deliberately de-centred our cyber strength: live security
scans became just ONE of three evidence connectors, not the headline.
Honest claim we make: "the engine transfers, the domain doesn't — we
built this loop for cyber compliance and pointed it at SEBI text."

Step 2 — We decided who the user is. The PS is written from the
broker's seat (RegTech). But a regulator is judging the hackathon, so
we added the free second window: the read-only SEBI Inspector view.
Same data, different lens.

Step 3 — We researched real, fresh scenarios (web research, primary
sources): on 3 July 2026 — nine days before our demo date — SEBI
actually amended Para 46 of the Master Circular, replacing how brokers
handle clients' unpaid securities with a pledge-based system called
CUSPA. Every broker in India is scrambling on this right now. That
became our hero scenario. Backdrop: SEBI also replaced the entire
Stock Brokers Regulations in Jan 2026, so "rules changing under your
feet" is a very live pain.

Step 4 — We researched how serious companies architect this, so a jury
can't call it a toy. Norm Ai (a US company that raised $120M+ to build
compliance agents for asset managers) compiles regulations into
structured logic and lets agents walk that structure step-by-step.
Anthropic's "Building Effective Agents" guidance says: in production,
use a fixed pipeline with AI only at the judgment steps — never
free-roaming agents — every step inspectable. That's exactly our
architecture: NEW REGULATION = NEW JOB THROUGH A FIXED PIPELINE, not
"new agent". The agents are disposable workers; the schema is the
permanent thing; a deterministic verifier checks everything; a human
signs. This is also the only architecture a regulator will trust.

Step 5 — For the hackathon we built a FRONTEND SANDBOX of the product:
every screen, running on realistic simulated data. The demo company is
"Walrus Securitas Broking Ltd" — a fictional small stock broker (SEBI
reg no INZ000247319, member NSE + BSE) — exactly the "smaller
intermediary with limited compliance resources" the PS names. The
in-app clock is pinned to 12 July 2026, so "SEBI amended this nine
days ago" stays true forever.

4. WHAT'S REAL AND WHAT'S SIMULATED (say this confidently, never hide it)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
- The regulatory scenario is REAL: the CUSPA amendment, its deadlines,
  the Master Circular structure. Clause text is a close paraphrase of
  public SEBI material, written for the demo.
- The data is SIMULATED but realistic: 33 obligations, 20 evidence
  artifacts, 9 tasks, 8 pipeline runs, 40 audit events, 6 scraper
  catches — all hand-built to be internally consistent.
- Everything is EXPLORABLE, nothing is ACTIONABLE: buttons that would
  change data show a small "sandbox" toast instead. That's deliberate.
- The black strip at the top of every page says it verbatim: "Sandbox
  environment — realistic simulated data · everything is explorable,
  nothing is actionable." Own it: the engine design is what we're
  showing, not fake live data.

5. MINI GLOSSARY (words you'll say a lot)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Circular        — a SEBI rule document (PDF).
Master Circular — the consolidated rulebook for one firm type.
Obligation      — one thing the firm must do, extracted from a clause.
Control         — the firm's internal process that satisfies it.
Evidence        — proof the control ran (document / data-check / scan).
Gap             — an obligation with no evidence. Orange in the UI.
Remediation     — the task that closes a gap.
Audit trail     — the append-only, hash-chained log of every action.
Agentic         — AI agents doing multi-step work (read → decide → act).
MCP             — Model Context Protocol; a standard port through which
                  any AI assistant can query a system. Ours is read-only.
CUSPA           — "Client Unpaid Securities Pledgee Account". New rule:
                  when a client hasn't fully paid for shares, the broker
                  no longer moves them to its own account — they stay in
                  the client's account, PLEDGED to the broker. Client
                  must be told by email/SMS; if unpaid after 5 trading
                  days the pledge is released/disposed on day 6; daily
                  reconciliation required. This is the Para 46 amendment.
VAPT / CSCRF    — security testing / SEBI's cybersecurity framework —
                  the ONE chapter where our evidence is a live scan.

PAGE 5 · EVIDENCE VAULT  (/evidence)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: compliance you can't PROVE doesn't exist. This is Pain #2.

SHOW: the three connector types across the grid: a DOCUMENT (the
board resolution), a DATA-CHECK (the client-fund segregation check),
a LIVE SCAN (the Walrus security scan) → open one artifact: hash,

point at a CUSPA obligation in the orange GAP state — no evidence

SAY: "Every obligation needs proof, and proof comes in three flavours:
documents you upload once, data-checks the system re-runs against
other systems of record — like re-checking client-fund segregation
against the exchange's own reports — and for the cybersecurity
chapter, live scans from our own security engine, not screenshots.
Every artifact is fingerprinted and timestamped, and re-verification
appends to its history — so this is ongoing compliance, not a one-time
checklist. And the new CUSPA obligations that have no proof yet —
those are gaps, they're orange, and each one already has a task."

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
PAGE 6 · REMEDIATION  (/remediation)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: gaps don't sit in a report — they become work with owners and dates.

SHOW: the task list → toggle list ↔ kanban → open one CUSPA task and
trace the chain printed on the card: task ← obligation ← clause.

SAY: "Nine tasks in flight — seven of them created by the pipeline
within minutes of the amendment landing. Look at any card: it tells
you which obligation it closes and which paragraph of the circular
demands it — no task exists here without a line of law behind it. And
every task is due BEFORE the circular's own phase deadline —
front-loaded on purpose, so there's room to verify before SEBI's date
arrives. The compliance officer stops being a PDF-reader and becomes
someone who just clears a queue."

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
PAGE 7 · AGENT CONSOLE  (/agents)  — the trust page
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: kills the "can you trust an AI with compliance?" question before
the jury asks it.

SHOW: the run list (RUN-041 to 048) → open RUN-047, the amendment
run → step through the replay: thought → action → observation → the
five deterministic verifier checks, all green → the run status:
"awaiting compliance-officer approval."

SAY: "Every pipeline run is recorded and replayable, step by step —
what the agent read, what it decided, why. Then a verifier — plain
deterministic code, not AI — checks every proposal: citations must
resolve to real clauses, deadlines must parse, applicability must
match our registration. Five checks, five passes. And even then, the
run is holding at the last gate: a human hasn't approved it yet. This
is the architecture the serious players use — agents inside a fixed
pipeline, never roaming free — because it's the only one a regulator
can audit."

PAGE 8 · AUDIT TRAIL  (/audit)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: for a regulator, the audit trail IS the product.

SAY: "Every action you've watched today — the catch, the extraction,
