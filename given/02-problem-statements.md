## PS1 — AI-Driven Detection of Synthetic Media and Phishing Attacks in Securities Markets
`PS1_synthetic-media-phishing.pdf`

**Problem.** Generative AI has introduced a new class of threats: hyper-personalised LLM-crafted
phishing emails, synthetic voice calls impersonating executives/regulators, deepfake videos of CEOs /
CIOs / market experts, and AI-generated social media designed to manipulate retail investor behaviour —
hitting brokers, investors, and market infrastructure institutions, with retail and first-gen investors
most vulnerable. A parallel gap: **no reliable way to verify that a communication purportedly from SEBI,
an exchange, a listed company, or a registered intermediary is genuine.** Detection + authentication
together are a systemic market-integrity / investor-protection threat existing infra doesn't address.

**Desired outcome.** A solution that improves the ability of retail investors, intermediaries, or MIIs
to **identify, flag, or respond** to AI-generated threats. Specify target user, channel(s) addressed, and
give clear evidence of detection or authentication performance.

## PS2 — Agentic Compliance: From Regulatory Text to Operational Action ⭐ OUR PICK
`PS2_agentic-compliance.pdf`

**Problem.** The securities-market framework evolves continuously — SEBI issues circulars, master
circulars, notifications, and guidelines, each carrying obligations for one or more intermediary classes
(stockbrokers, depositories, AMCs, RTAs, investment advisers, MIIs). Two related challenges:
1. **Dynamic regulatory translation** — interpreting a new/amended requirement, mapping it to the affected
   intermediary's operational processes, and updating compliance workflows promptly and consistently.
   Today this is manual legal interpretation + circular-by-circular tracking → uneven implementation,
   delayed adaptation, divergent interpretations.
2. **Ongoing compliance management** — tracking existing obligations, mapping each to evidence of
   fulfilment, maintaining audit trails, and finding/remediating gaps before they become findings.
   Operationally intensive, manual, gap-prone — worst for smaller intermediaries.

Common root: **regulation exists as unstructured human-readable text, while operational systems need
structured, machine-actionable rules.** Bridging that gap — transforming regulatory intent into
programmable, **auditable** compliance logic — is the core unsolved problem.

**Desired outcome.** A technology-based solution that bridges regulatory text → operational compliance
action. It should demonstrably reduce the gap between regulatory issuance and operational action, or
materially improve the **efficiency, accuracy, and auditability** of compliance management for
intermediaries. Solutions should **specify the intermediary category and regulatory corpus** worked with,
and demonstrate performance on **at least one concrete regulatory scenario**.

**Suggested regulatory corpus.** SEBI's publicly available **master circulars for stockbrokers and/or
investment advisers** — they consolidate all applicable obligations in a structured format and are free
on SEBI's website.

## PS3 — Super App for Unified Multi-Asset Investing & Awareness for Retail Investors
`PS3_super-app-multi-asset.pdf`

**Problem.** Two compounding barriers for India's retail investors: (1) holdings fragmented across
multiple demat/trading accounts and brokers — no single consolidated view of total holdings, exposure,
risk (Account Aggregator + the SEBI-NSDL-CDSL Unified Investor Platform exist as building blocks, but no
integrated analytics-rich cross-asset experience does); (2) participation skewed to equities, with limited
awareness/access to REITs, InvITs, corporate bonds, other fixed-income — investor understanding lags
product innovation → mis-selling and shallow participation. Net: sophisticated portfolio intelligence +
multi-asset access remain the preserve of institutions / HNIs.

**Desired outcome.** A secure **investor super app** consolidating holdings across depositories, brokers,
and asset classes into one intelligent dashboard, while expanding awareness of and access to alternate
instruments — combining portfolio aggregation, risk/exposure analytics, transaction intelligence with
interactive product education, risk profiling, suitability assessment, and seamless multi-asset access.

## PS4 — Simplifying IPO Offer Document Preparation for SMEs
`PS4_ipo-sme-offer-doc.pdf`

**Problem.** SME participation in public markets is limited by the complexity and cost of preparing an
**IPO offer document** — extensive merchant-banker / legal / compliance involvement, months of work,
costs disproportionate to capital raised. Lean-team SME promoters with limited capital-market exposure
are heavily reliant on intermediaries from the outset.

**Desired outcome.** A solution enabling an SME promoter to generate a **substantially complete draft IPO
offer document** — cutting prep time, lowering early-stage intermediary dependence, covering all material
disclosure requirements under SEBI's SME IPO framework, flagging gaps/inconsistencies, simple enough for a
first-time issuer, while **preserving authorised intermediaries' role in review/certification** before
regulatory submission.
